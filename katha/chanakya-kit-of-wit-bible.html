Section 01

Foundation & the big idea

Folk with Folks is the studio. Katha is the product line — do-it-yourself puppet kits that turn Indian folk characters into something a child builds, performs, and keeps.

The product in one line

A clamshell "pencil-box" that opens into a tiny puppet stage, holding everything a 9–14-year-old needs to build a wire-armature Chanakya puppet and stage their own scene of cleverness.

The theme & base

The base is Indian folk storytelling — the same well that feeds Panchatantra, Tenali Rama, Akbar–Birbal, and Chanakya. Katha doesn't retell these as a screen experience; it puts the making and the performing back into the child's hands. The recurring promise across the whole line is: you build the character, then you make it speak.

For this first kit, the chosen character is Chanakya and the chosen theme is wit and strategy — hence "Kit of Wit." Where other folk characters lean on humour or mischief, Chanakya gives us the angle of the clever long-game: planning, patience, and outsmarting a bigger opponent. That theme runs from the name, into the included wisdom cards, into the scenes the manual suggests.

Who it's for

The child (9–14)

Old enough to handle wire and clay with focus, young enough to still love making a character talk. Gets a craft project, a toy, and a story they can perform.

The gift-buyer (parent / relative)

Wants screen-free, made-in-India, culturally rooted, "actually does something" gifting. The conversion-into-a-stage is the hook that makes them pick it up.

The spine of the brand

The primary brand symbol is The Torana Stage — the temple-gateway arch that is simultaneously the logo motif and the literal shape the kit opens into. The arch is the throughline: it appears on the box, becomes the proscenium of the stage, anchors the website, and frames the YouTube channel. Lock this single idea and everything downstream stays coherent.

Section 02

The character: Chanakya

Condensed from the full Chanakya character bible. Enough to build him, dress him, and write his voice — without re-opening the long document.

Who he is, and why him

Chanakya (also Kautilya) is the Mauryan-era teacher and strategist — the mind behind the rise of Chandragupta Maurya and the author of the Arthashastra. For a kit about wit, he's the ideal anchor: the small, plainly-dressed man who out-thinks kings. That contrast — humble appearance, formidable intellect — is exactly what makes a good puppet and a good story for this age group.

Physical appearance

  • Lean, wiry build — not a hero's physique. The cleverness is the power.
  • Shaven head with a shikha (the single knotted tuft) — instantly readable silhouette, easy to sculpt.
  • Sharp, watchful eyes; a slight, knowing set to the mouth.
  • Often barefoot or in simple sandals — a teacher, not a courtier.

Mauryan-era clothing

GarmentNote for the puppet
Dhoti / antariyaLower unstitched cloth, wrapped and tucked. In undyed cotton or pale natural tone.
UttariyaUpper shawl draped over one shoulder — the main place to introduce the indigo accent.
YajnopavitaThe sacred thread across the torso — a fine cream cord, a nice detail step for older kids.
Minimal jewelleryHe's austere by design. Keep ornamentation almost nil — this also keeps the kit cheap and simple.

His voice (for the manual & wit cards)

Calm, dry, a little sly. He answers a problem with a question. He prefers the indirect move to the loud one. The "wit cards" in the kit should sound like small, repeatable maxims — practical cleverness a child can actually use, drawn in spirit from the Arthashastra's strategic thinking but written plainly and warmly, never preachy.

Section 03

Format & the conversion

The whole product idea lives or dies on one moment: the box opening into a stage. This is the section to protect.

The object

  • Form: rigid clamshell, the size and feel of a premium pencil box.
  • Dimensions: 220 × 90 × 35 mm (closed).
  • Closed state: reads as a beautiful, giftable object — a folk artefact, not a toy carton.

The conversion — how the box becomes the stage

1

Unlatch & open flat

The clamshell opens on its long hinge and lays flat, doubling the footprint into a low, wide playing field.

2

The lid stands up as the Torana

The inside of the lid carries the printed Torana arch backdrop. A scored fold and a tab-and-slot lets it lock upright as the proscenium — the literal stage frame and brand symbol, now in 3D.

3

The base becomes the floor

The base tray, emptied of its die-cut puppet-and-prop compartments, is the stage floor. Interchangeable backdrop cards drop in behind the puppet.

4

The puppet takes the stage

The finished wire-armature Chanakya stands (or is tie-down anchored) on the floor. The child now performs — and the box that arrived as a gift is now a working theatre.

Design rule: the conversion must be doable by a 10-year-old with no glue and no adult, in under a minute, every single time, reversibly. If a fold needs force or tape, redesign it.

Does it convert into something? — yes, and that's the pitch

"Pencil-box → puppet stage" is the one sentence that sells the kit cold. Closed, it earns shelf and gift appeal; open, it earns play and repeat use. Keep this transformation on the front of the box, the first frame of every Reel, and the hero of the website.

Section 04

What's in the kit

Everything the box holds, grouped by what it does. The die-cut tray gives each item a home — which itself becomes part of the unboxing delight.

A. To build the puppet

  • Pre-cut aluminium armature wire (body + limbs), gauges chosen for child-safe bending.
  • Air-dry / epoxy clay sachets for head, hands, and feet — or a pre-cast Chanakya head option for the "easy" path.
  • Cotton wadding / batting for bulking the torso and limbs.
  • Fine binding thread and a blunt needle.
  • Pre-cut fabric pieces: dhoti (natural), uttariya (indigo), and a fine cord for the sacred thread.

B. To stage the story

  • The lid's printed Torana proscenium (built into the box).
  • 2–3 interchangeable backdrop cards (e.g. a court, a study, a road).
  • A few flat prop punch-outs (scroll, lamp, a second silhouette to argue with).

C. To carry the theme — "Wit"

  • A small deck of Niti / wit cards — short Chanakya-flavoured maxims and "what would Chanakya do?" prompts that double as scene starters.

D. To guide the child

  • The printed manual: who Chanakya was, the step-by-step build, the conversion, and 2–3 ready-to-perform mini-scenes.
  • A QR link to the YouTube build-along and a teaser, tying physical to the channel.

Section 06

Making the puppet — full procedure

The wire-armature construction sequence, written as the manual would teach it. This is the spine of the build-along video too.

1

Form the armature skeleton

Take the pre-cut aluminium wire. Fold the long body wire in half to make the spine and legs; twist a second piece across the top for shoulders and arms. You now have a stick-figure skeleton — twist the joins tight so they hold a pose.

2

Set the proportions

Bend to Chanakya's lean proportions: a slightly shorter, grounded figure. Make sure he can stand. Loop the feet flat so he balances (or so the stage tie-downs can anchor him).

3

Bulk the body

Wrap cotton wadding around torso and limbs, binding it down with thread so it doesn't unravel. Keep the arms thin and the torso modest — austere, not heroic.

4

Sculpt or fit the head

Maker path: shape the head from clay — shaven dome, the shikha tuft, sharp brow, knowing mouth — and press it onto the neck wire while soft. Easy path: push the pre-cast head onto the neck wire. Add clay hands and feet the same way.

5

Let it cure

Set the clay aside to dry/cure per the sachet. (In the video, this is the "meanwhile, let's prep the stage" cutaway.)

6

Dress him

Wrap the natural dhoti low and tuck it; drape the indigo uttariya over one shoulder; tie the fine cord as the sacred thread across the torso. A dab of glue or a stitch holds the cloth.

7

Finish the face

Paint or ink the eyes and mouth — the single most expression-defining step. Keep it minimal: Chanakya's look is in the eyes.

8

Pose & stage him

Because the armature holds pose, set him mid-thought — a raised hand, a turned head. Convert the box to the Torana Stage, drop in a backdrop, and he's ready to perform.

Authoring note: write each step so it has a clear "done" check ("he should now stand on his own"). For 9–14, a visible milestone every step is what keeps them building to the end.

Section 08

How to sell it

Two products from one design: the physical kit, and the digital "home-print" version. Sell both, on different channels, at different price logics.

Physical kit

  • Own site (folkwithfolks.in): best margin, full brand control, the home base.
  • Etsy: reaches gifting + handmade + craft-kit buyers, including export.
  • Indian marketplaces: reach + trust for domestic buyers; mind the fees.

Digital / print-at-home

  • Gumroad: instant delivery of the printable kit + manual; zero inventory.
  • Canva-based products: templated printables and activity sheets as low-cost entry products and lead magnets.

Pricing logic (to be locked)

  • Physical kit priced as a premium gift, not a commodity toy — the conversion-into-stage and the handmade craft justify it.
  • Digital print-at-home as a low-friction entry product that can later upsell to the physical box.
  • Build the price up from real costs: box, materials, print, packing, channel fee, and your time — then set the gift-worthy number above that floor.

Compliance to keep in view

For selling in and out of India, keep Goods and Services Tax (GST) registration and, for exports, the Letter of Undertaking (LUT) in mind — these came up in the digital-product research and affect both marketplace setup and pricing. (This is a planning note, not financial or legal advice — confirm specifics with a qualified professional.)

Section 09

How to market it

Lead with the transformation and the craft. The product is inherently visual and inherently shareable — let the format do the selling.

Story-first content

  • Instagram Reels (@folkwithfolks): the 15-second teaser using the real Chanakya clay puppet as image-to-video reference, retimed stop-motion-on-twos. First frame = the box opening into the stage.
  • YouTube channel: long-form build-alongs ("make Chanakya with me"), the channel branding and banner already set up. The kit's QR points here.
  • The conversion clip: one reusable hero shot — box → stage → puppet performing — cut for Reels, YouTube intro, and the website hero.

On-ground & experiential

  • Café workshops: run the build as a paid weekend session — proof, content, and sales in one room.
  • Venue outreach: the BookMyShow-style one-pager to pitch workshop slots at cafés and event spaces.

Engagement & retention

  • Katha Quest dashboard: the gamified XP / boss-stage tracker turns "I built one puppet" into "I want the next character" — the bridge from one kit to a collectible line.
  • The line promise: market Chanakya as the first of a cast (Tenali Rama next, etc.) so each release compounds the last.

Section 11

Open decisions

The handful of things still genuinely undecided. Resolve these and the kit is ready to manufacture and list.

  1. Price points — physical kit and digital version, built up from real costs.
  2. Head: cast vs sculpt — do you ship a pre-cast Chanakya head, the clay-to-sculpt path, or both in one box?
  3. Manufacturer & minimum order — who prints/assembles the clamshell, and at what run size.
  4. First batch size — workshop-and-presell to fund the run, or commit to a small inventory first.
  5. Wit-card content — final set of Chanakya maxims/prompts, written in his voice.
  6. Launch sequence — Day 1 is the day you actually begin; sequence teaser → site → presell → workshop from there.
